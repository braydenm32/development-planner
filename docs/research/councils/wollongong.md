## Battle-axe lots

A battle-axe lot reaches the street only by an access handle (a strip, often with a right of carriageway). The rules treat it differently on each pathway:

- **Housing Code (CDC):** the access handle is left out of the lot area (cl 3.9(2)). The battle-axe test is about the lot's shape, not where the house goes: "the lot must be at least 12m by 12m (not including the access laneway) and must have an access laneway that is at least 3m wide" (cl 3.1(3)(f), **read** Aug 2025). The handle's width is on the deposited plan. The lot has **three side boundaries and a rear boundary**, the rear being opposite the boundary the house faces (cl 3.3(2)). Instead of a street setback, the house must be at least **3 m from the rear boundary of the lot in front** (cl 3.10(15)), and battle-axe houses don't count in the neighbours' front setback average (cl 3.10(2)(a)). Parking must let cars **leave forwards** (cl 3.16(3)). Nothing can be built over a registered easement (cl 3.2(1)(d)).
- **No one-storey limit on the CDC route.** The Housing Code covers "a new 1 or 2 storey dwelling house" (cl 3.1(1)(a)) up to 8.5 m (cl 3.8), with no storey rule for battle-axe lots; a basement or basement garage counts as a storey (cl 3.1(2)). The DCP's one-storey default for battle-axe lots (B1 4.1.2(1)) is a DA control: council DCPs are a consideration for development applications under s 4.15(1)(a)(iii) of the EP&A Act, and a CDC is assessed only against the Codes SEPP standards (**unverified**; no source read says so outright, but it's how the two pathways are set up).
- **DCP (DA):** B1 4.1.2(1) limits battle-axe lots to **one storey** unless the objectives are met, so a two-storey design on the DA route needs a case against those objectives. Whether B1 leaves the handle out of the site area for site coverage and landscaping wasn't checked, so the checker shows both the area without the handle and the title area for the DCP.
- **Lot width** for the side setback bands is "measured at the building line". On a battle-axe lot which side that is isn't obvious, so confirm it with council or the certifier; the app asks for it under Lot facts. The Housing Code side setback then applies on all three side boundaries.
- **Right of carriageway:** if the handle carries one for a neighbour, the house and driveway have to keep it clear.
- **Easements:** their purpose and width come from the title's section 88B instrument. Drainage and services easements are normally no-build; the checker should treat each one as a restricted area until its terms are known.
- **Trees:** declared trees 8 m or taller can't be removed under a CDC (cl 3.33(1)); removal needs the DA. Under a CDC the house must also stay **3 m from every protected tree's trunk**, on the lot and the neighbours' (cl 3.33(2)). Details in [Trees](#trees-chapter-e17-and-as-4970) below.

### Dictionary terms the checker needs

- **Building height**: "the vertical distance from ground level (existing) to the highest point of the building", including plant and lift overruns but not chimneys, flues, antennae and the like. **Ground level (existing)**: "the existing level of a site at any point". So height is measured straight down at each point to the natural surface, which the app's survey terrain gives.
- **Gross floor area**: to the internal face of external walls at 1.4 m above the floor. Includes mezzanines and habitable rooms in a basement or attic. Excludes stairs and lifts, basement storage and services, plant rooms, "car parking to meet any requirements of the consent authority", terraces and balconies with outer walls under 1.4 m, and voids. The Housing Code's own definition differs (it excludes one car space up to 18 m²).
- **Storey**: between one floor and the next, not counting a space with only a lift, stair or meter room, a mezzanine or an attic. **Basement**: floor mostly below existing ground, with the storey above less than 1 m above existing ground. **Attic**: habitable space wholly within the roof above the ceiling line.
- **Site coverage**: share of the site area covered by buildings, not counting basements, awnings at the street or boundary, eaves, and unenclosed balconies, decks and pergolas. **Landscaped area**: used for growing plants, grasses and trees, not including any building, structure or hard paving.
- **Site area**: the land the development is carried out on. **Battle-axe lot**: "a lot that has access to a road by an access laneway".
- **Secondary dwelling**: self-contained, with a principal dwelling on the same lot, and inside, attached to or separate from it.

For the actual block, the zone, height and FSR should come from the Planning Portal's controls lookup (see [../map-placement.md](../map-placement.md)).

### Trees (Chapter E17 and AS 4970)

From [Chapter E17](https://www.wollongong.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/8983/Wollongong-DCP-2009-Chapter-E17-Preservation-and-Management-of-Trees-and-Vegetation.pdf) (Rev 3, in force 1 Jul 2023, **read**), council's [Tree Management Policy](https://wollongong.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/193267/Tree-Management-web.pdf) (adopted 15 Dec 2025, **read**) and the [trees and development page](https://www.wollongong.nsw.gov.au/residents/trees-and-your-property/trees-and-development) (**read**).

| Section | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Definitions; Policy s3 | A **declared tree** is "five (5) metres or more in height" or has "a diameter of 30 cm (300mm) or more measured at ground level". Native or exotic alike. Section 8.2(1) also declares every tree that isn't on the exempt list, which reads wider than the definition; ask council if it matters. Dead or dying trees need a permit too (11.2). |
| 10(f), Appendix 1 | Exempt species need no approval (for example camphor laurel, privet, liquidambar, umbrella tree, cotoneaster, domestic fruit trees), except in heritage areas, as threatened species habitat, or where a consent condition or 88B restriction keeps them. |
| 8.1, 8.3, 11.4 | Removing or pruning a declared tree needs a development consent or a council permit. When the work is for a new house, it goes in the **DA**: "Permits are not suitable as a means to facilitate development, complying or otherwise." |
| 10(b), 11.7(a) | No council approval is needed for tree work under a CDC that meets the Codes SEPP, but removal that can't meet the Codes SEPP needs a DA. Council's page says to check with them before tree work on a CDC. |
| Housing Code cl 3.33 (Aug 2025, **read**) | A CDC covers removing a tree only if it's not on council's significant tree register, within 3 m of a building over 25 m², and **under 8 m tall** (for a new house). And development under a CDC must be **at least 3 m from each protected tree** on the lot and adjoining lots, measured from the base of the trunk; driveways, paths, paving, ramps, awnings and pool fences can be closer if they cut or fill no more than 150 mm. **This is the 3 m trunk setback**, and it's a CDC rule, not a DCP one. |
| **13.3** | **The 3 m rule:** "Where Council has issued a Development Consent for a structure or building, any tree with its base within three (3) metres of that building or structure on the subject land may be removed without further application to Council, provided the Council's Tree Management Officer is satisfied before the tree is removed that its base is within the three (3) metre limit." It's a removal allowance once a DA is approved, measured from the trunk base to the approved building. It isn't a protection distance, and it doesn't mean building 3 m from a kept tree is fine on the DA route (compare the Housing Code's 3 m setback above, which applies to CDCs). |
| 13.1 | For a DA with trees on the site, council decides whether each tree stays, goes, or the house and driveway layout changes. The DA needs an arborist report. Council's page also asks for trees on neighbouring land to be shown. |
| Drawing 7000-LD01 (Dec 2019, **read**) | Fencing to the **drip line** (canopy edge) or as an AQF 5 arborist directs; "final extent of TPZ to be determined by an AQF level 5 arborist for every site". Hold point: "No excavation is to occur within the tree protection zone", and no level changes, storage, fires or attachments in it. Trunk protection at least 2 m high; ground protection where machines must cross. Cites AS 4970-2009 with Amendment 1. |
| 13.2, 13.4 | A tree to be kept is assessed to AS 4970-2009 (the DCP misprints it "AS4790-2009"). A **tree protection zone** is fenced for the whole build and kept free of "any buildings, footings, excavation, retaining walls, materials storage, services, level changes or hard surfaces". An AQF level 5 arborist certifies it before work starts, midway on long jobs, and at completion. |
| 9; Policy | Removed trees are replaced: the Policy makes replacement on private land "mandatory" (a voucher from council's nursery with a permit), and E17 s9 allows replacement planting, a fee, or planting elsewhere as an offset. |
| 11.2 | A tree on the boundary belongs to the side holding most of the trunk at ground level. Street trees are council's; none of the pages covered driveway crossings near street trees. |

**AS 4970**, which sets the protection zones:

- **2009 edition** (what E17 names). TPZ radius = **12 × trunk diameter at 1.4 m** (DBH), at least 2 m and at most 15 m (**secondary**, [Eurobodalla council](https://www.esc.nsw.gov.au/property/vegetation-and-tree-removal/protecting-trees-on-development-sites) and a contractor's page). Up to **10% of the TPZ area** can be encroached if the structural root zone (SRZ) isn't touched. More than 10% needs detailed investigation by an AQF level 5 arborist (**secondary**, [Inner West council's technical manual](https://www.innerwest.nsw.gov.au/ArticleDocuments/739/Tree%20Management%20Technical%20Manual.pdf.aspx), sections 2.8 and 6.6). SRZ radius = (D × 50)^0.42 × 0.64, with D the trunk diameter just above the root buttress, and at least 1.5 m (**unverified**; widely used but not found in a page read today). Encroaching on the SRZ is "not likely to be supported" without root investigation (same manual, 2.9).
- **2025 edition.** AS 4970-2025 superseded the 2009 edition on 30 May 2025 (**secondary**, [Sparke Helmore, 6 Aug 2025](https://www.sparke.com.au/insights/important-updates-to-as49702025-protection-of-trees-on-development-sites/)). The root area is now called the **nominal root zone (NRZ)**, and "TPZ" means the above-ground protection only. Encroachment up to **20%** is "moderate", and 20 to 30% is "major" and needs root mapping and tree-sensitive design. Whether the NRZ formula changed from 12 × DBH wasn't found. An Amendment 1 (2025) exists; its content wasn't found. E17 predates it, so ask council which edition its arborists assess against.
- "RPZ" isn't a term in either edition. Root protection zone is a generic name for what AS 4970 calls the TPZ (2009) or NRZ (2025).

A worked example by the 2009 formulas (trunk diameter taken as DBH; the SRZ uses the trunk diameter too, so it's a lower bound):

| Trunk Ø | TPZ radius (12 × Ø) | TPZ area | 10% of it | SRZ radius (approx.) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.3 m | 3.6 m | 41 m² | 4 m² | 2.0 m |
| 0.5 m | 6.0 m | 113 m² | 11 m² | 2.5 m |
| 0.7 m | 8.4 m | 222 m² | 22 m² | 2.8 m |
| 0.9 m | 10.8 m | 366 m² | 37 m² | 3.2 m |

A tree 5 m or taller is a declared tree by height alone, and one 8 m or taller can't be removed under a CDC. A large tree's TPZ can be more than three times the 3 m distance. Council's default fence line (LD01) is the drip line, half the canopy spread, which for a large tree often sits inside the AS 4970 TPZ, so the arborist's TPZ, not the drip line, is the safer limit for the checker.

What the checker should do: draw each tree's TPZ and SRZ, measure how much of each TPZ the house, slab, driveway, retaining walls, batters and services take, and flag more than 10% (2009) or 20% (2025), any SRZ encroachment, and trees within 3 m of the house (removable after a DA consent, subject to the council officer).

## Gaps for Wollongong

1. The **LEP map values** for the lot (height, FSR, acid sulfate soils class, and whether the biodiversity, riparian, escarpment or foreshore maps touch it). The current LEP text is read (2 Oct 2026 version).
2. Whether council's records flag the lot under **Chapter E12** (the section 10.7 certificate or council can say); E12 itself is read and has no numeric trigger.
3. The **street side of the crossing**: the footpath width, kerb type and gutter level at the handle, which C33's heights hang off (the survey or council's road levels).
4. Which crossover width applies (B1 says 3 m maximum; the driveway page says 3 to 5.5 m).
5. The lot's real **zone, height and FSR** from the Planning Portal once the survey is placed, and whether any overlay (flood, escarpment, bushfire, heritage) applies.
6. The **suburb character statement** in D1 and any Part D precinct chapter.
7. **Battle-axe details**: the handle's width (deposited plan), whether a right of carriageway runs over it (88B instrument), and how B1 measures site area and front setback on a battle-axe lot.
8. The **easement terms** (purpose, width, who benefits) from the section 88B instrument.
9. **Trees**: the species and where the trunk diameters were measured (for the exempt list and DBH), which AS 4970 edition council's arborists now use, the 2025 NRZ formula, and whether council keeps a significant tree register.
